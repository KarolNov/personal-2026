I joined MarkUp.io early and spent four years on it. The product lets you drop comments straight onto a live website,
which meant learning the limits of the web the hard way.

<Figure src={review} alt="MarkUp.io open on ceros.com: three comments in the sidebar, numbered pins on the page and a new comment being typed over a card.">
  Reviewing a live site: comments pin to the element they're about, and the thread sits alongside.
</Figure>

## What I did

- **Made third-party sites commentable.** Proxying and shimming other people's websites into iframes, pins that stay
  stuck to the element they were left on as the page reflows, and a Chrome extension for the pages that refuse to be
  embedded at all.
- **Built a native screenshot pipeline.** Snapshot the page, rebuild it, render it in a headless browser. Back then, it
  beat every HTML-to-image library we tried on consistency.
- **Helped architect notifications**, both instant and digest, for email, Slack and Teams, on queues (BullMQ, SQS) so
  a burst of comments never held up the app.

## What I took from it

Building from customer evidence: analytics, customer learning sessions and a Slack community where you see the effect
of a fix the same day. It's the habit I've kept most from those years.
