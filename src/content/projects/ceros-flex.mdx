Flex is the rebuild of Ceros Studio: a client-heavy visual editor where people design interactive content together,
online or offline, and can step back through its history. I joined it as the lead of a team of up to eight senior
engineers, and today work on its architecture as an individual contributor reporting to the CTO.

## What I did

- **Set the frontend architecture.** Real-time collaboration with offline editing and version history on Y.js, an
  extensible visual HTML editor built on Web Components, and a DSL that can be edited both visually and as code. That
  last one made adding AI collaborators straightforward: they edit the same document people do.
- **Moved the sync engine to intent-based operations.** Two-way syncing became an operations pipeline where every
  change is validated, normalised and auto-fixed before it lands, so documents stay consistent across collaborators,
  offline edits and version history.
- **Shaped how the DOM tree lives in the CRDT**, with fractional indexing so concurrent moves stay conflict-free and
  cheap to observe on the client.
- **Let customers bring Flex content into their own sites and apps** without relying on iframes.
- **Worked on performance across the stack.** A few recent changes made server-side and static rendering around 30%
  faster.
- **Testing at scale.** With QE and DevOps, an end-to-end pipeline of around 10,000 Playwright tests on custom AWS
  runners, in 6–8 minutes on every PR.
- **Grew people.** Helped my reports grow into staff and lead roles, and helped reshape engineering culture at Ceros
  around quality, security and speed of delivery in the age of AI-assisted development.
